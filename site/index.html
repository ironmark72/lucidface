LucidFace

Observation-locked restoration of out-of-focus faces in interview video. The restoration may add only what the camera could not record.

Code Technical report Benchmark Method

Abstract

A missed focus pull leaves the background sharp and the speaker soft for the whole take. Video upscalers barely help: the frame is not under-sampled, it is low-passed by the lens. Face models rebuild a convincing face, but they also rewrite what the camera did record, such as where the person is looking or the colour of their eyes.

LucidFace pins the restored face to the observed frame below a cutoff frequency (consistency lock), blends only fine detail over time along trustworthy optical flow (temporal steadier), leaves in-focus shots untouched (focus gate), and flags and fades out doubtful frames. On a reproducible benchmark built from public-domain interviews it halves perceptual distance to the sharp original and raises identity similarity in 13 of 15 cases. We also report what did not work.

The problem

Tried first

Upscalers do not fix defocus

On a 27-minute defocused interview, a state-of-the-art video restorer (SeedVR2, 3B and 7B, input pre-shrunk 1x to 4x) only slightly crisped the eyes. Defocus removes high frequencies from a face that already has enough pixels.

Then

Face models invent, including the eyes

A face prior (CodeFormer) rebuilt eyes, brows and lips. At its usual setting it also turned one brown iris grey and pointed that eye at the camera while the other looked away. Viewers notice eyes first.

Method

1 · The consistency lock

A defocused lens keeps the low frequencies of the face: iris position and colour, gaze, shading. LucidFace keeps exactly those from the camera and takes only the high band from the model: r′ = r + Gs * (a − r), with the cutoff tied to the eye distance. It is closed-form and works with any face model.

2 · The temporal steadier

Per-frame restoration shimmers. Restored detail is blended with the previous frame's, moved along optical flow, and only where the flow is trustworthy, so blinks and speech are never ghosted. Low frequencies stay locked to the current frame, so motion never lags.

3 · The focus gate

Face models degrade faces that are already sharp. A scale-free focus score separates sharp from defocused faces with a clear gap; only shots below the threshold are restored.

4 · Quality control with graceful fallback

Frames where the model departed from the footage, eyes shimmer, identity drifts or tracking is unreliable are flagged, faded back to the original, and listed with timestamps in an HTML report.

5 · Built for real files

Only the face box travels to the GPU and back, and it is composited onto the untouched original at the same bit depth, with the original audio and timecode.

What did not work

The lock does not improve averaged scores on synthetic blur. It was built for a failure seen on real camera defocus (rewritten gaze and iris colour) that synthetic blur on clean footage did not trigger. It stays on as a guarantee; --lock 0 turns it off.

Restoration adds flicker. Every restored version flickers more against the ground truth than the blurry input does. The steadier reduces it but does not close the gap.

Colour bias in this run. A colour-matrix bug, since fixed and tested, biased these comparisons against restoration, so the wins above are lower bounds. Full details in the benchmark.

Get started

git clone https://github.com/ironmark72/lucidface && cd lucidface
bash scripts/pod_setup.sh ~/models
export LUCIDFACE_CODEFORMER_DIR=~/models/CodeFormer
lucidface run interview.mp4 -o interview_fixed.mov

You get the restored video and an HTML QC report. Requires a CUDA GPU with 16 GB or more. The code is MIT; the default face model (CodeFormer) is non-commercial, see NOTICE. Restored faces are estimates, not evidence: do not use them for identification.
